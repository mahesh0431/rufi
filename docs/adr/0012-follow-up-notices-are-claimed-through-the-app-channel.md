# 0012 · Follow-up notices are claimed through the app's channel, best effort

**Date:** 11 Oct 2026 · **Status:** accepted

When a follow-up comes due, the Rufi app tells the person with a Mac notice. The core owns every health rule, so it decides what is due: one function, `classify` (`core/record/due.ts`), on the Mac's local time, never through UTC, used by the Follow-ups page, Needs you and the notices. A date is due during its year, month or day (a time: from that time to the end of its day) and overdue after it; "at the next visit" and no date are never due. The Electron main process owns only the wake-up and the notice: at core ready, every 15 minutes (an unref'd interval) and on `powerMonitor` `resume`, it sends `claim-notices` on the private IPC channel it already has to the core it started (`@rufi/contracts/app`), and gets back `notices`: per person, the counts of follow-ups that just became due or overdue, with no title or name. The claim (`core/record/notices.ts`) is one write transaction: it selects the open, unremoved follow-ups that are due or overdue and whose marker in `follow_up_notices` is for another due or a lower state, upserts each marker to `(due, state)`, and commits before it answers. So each state of each due is told once, also after a restart; a moved date is told again without the save writer resetting anything; a Rufi that starts after the month tells only "overdue"; a clock moved back never lowers a marker. The marker is operational state in its own table (migration 3): not the record, not in its history, not counted in the record version, and never written by the save writer. The channel needs no session: only the core's own parent can use it. One claim runs at a time, with a deadline; a failed or unanswered one is logged and the next tick tries again. The notice text is neutral ("Rufi · 1 follow-up is due, 2 are overdue"); its click focuses the window and loads `/people/<id>/follow-ups` on the same origin, or opens a window on today's fresh launch link with that path set by main. The guarantee is "attempted", not "seen": the marker is committed before the notice shows, so a crash between them loses that notice, and a notice hidden by Focus or turned off isn't tried again; Needs you is the authority and is right on its next read. Considered: a core timer that pushes a stream line (main doesn't read the stream, and a state taken with no listener is lost for good, also in a browser-only session); main signing in over HTTP to claim by POST (it would work when the app attaches to a Rufi started in a terminal, but main would hold a session and proof for a developer path); a renderer bridge (nothing is told with no window open); marker columns on `follow_ups` (timer noise in the record's history); OS-scheduled notifications (native code, and stale data while Rufi is quit). The cost: no notices in attached mode or in a browser, a restored older backup can repeat a notice given after it, and a second channel (calendar, phone) gets its own marker rather than this one.
