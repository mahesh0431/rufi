# 0013 · Results have a category: lab or measurement

**Date:** 11 Oct 2026 · **Status:** accepted; Lab results lists tests, not reports, since ADR 0028 (1 Nov 2026)

A lab test and a measurement are different things to read: a lab test comes from a report and is read report by report; a measurement (blood pressure, pulse, weight, height, temperature, SpO2) is taken often and read day by day, and Apple Health will bring many. So the record has two pages for results, **Lab results** and **Measurements**, but one kind of record: every result stays a `result` in the one `results` table, with a required `category` column, `lab` or `measurement` (migration 4, `CHECK` and `NOT NULL`). The two pages are page ids, `lab_result` and `measurement`, that the read model (`core/record/views.ts`) filters by category; tiles, sidebar counts and a test's series are per category, so a lab test and a measurement of the same name are two series. Lab results group by report, and a report's lab results come whole; a report's measurements go to Measurements, under the day each was taken, at the precision known. Pointers, sources, history, chips and follow-up links keep `{kind: result, id}`, so nothing that points at a result changes, and `isCurrent` doesn't look at the category: an amended result in the other category still hides the one it replaces.

The AI chooses the category with the person when it saves, as it decides everything that is judgement; code never works it out from the test's name or from having a report. Every added result needs one; there is no default. An amended result, typed or a document's correction, takes the category of the result it replaces when the save leaves it out (the required check runs after that), and gives another only when the person agreed. A document item saved as another source must not give one: another source never changes the saved result, and moving it is a second save. Moving a result is an ordinary change of its `category` on the same id, with the person's words, a receipt and history, and undo is the reverse change. That is the one change a result read from a report can take in place: what it prints and its date still change only by amendment (ADR 0005), and a change that mixes the category with a printed field or date is refused whole.

A measurement's date is when it was taken. A document item saved as a measurement takes only its own date (the reader gives one when the page dates that measurement, such as a blood pressure taken at a visit) or the person's, never the report's first printed date, which is the lab's collection date; with neither, the save is refused with words about when it was measured. The reader copies measurements as it copies lab results and doesn't classify them; a blood pressure stays its printed value, such as "130/85".

Results saved before migration 4 get `lab`. That is a compatibility value from before real data existed, not a judgement: all checked-in fictional data were lab results, a fictional folder's measurements can be moved in the chat, and old history snapshots and receipts stay as they were, so no category change is invented in history. A restored older backup is upgraded the same way.

Considered: two record kinds on one table (the identity of saved results would change, sources, history, chips, follow-up links and citations would need aliases, and an amendment couldn't cross categories); a nullable category (a third state, rows on neither page); a permanent `lab` default (a new save could miss the choice silently); classifying by test name in code (people's words are the AI's to read). The cost: every save of a result names a category, a category change can move a row during paging (an accepted race, visible on reload), and results of an old fictional folder all start on Lab results.
