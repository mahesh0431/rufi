# 0026 · Agents start sub-agents

**Date:** 30 Oct 2026 · **Status:** accepted

An agent in Rufi's chat starts agents of its own with Rufi's `start_agent` tool, for a task that can run on its own: a **sub-agent**, with a title and its whole task as its first message. It runs at once and goes on after the reply that started it ends. `agent_status` says how the agents below the caller are (no wait yet), and `read_agent` reads the caller's own conversation or one below it, a page of turns at a time. A sub-agent can start its own, to any depth. It is the one kind: Codex's own sub-agents (`spawn_agent` and its tools) are off in the chat's Codex (`[agents] enabled = false`, `multi_agent_v2 = false`), as they are for the reader. These are tools of the agent system (ADR 0024): Rufi's chat does them there (`server/sub-agents.ts`), and screens don't show sub-agents yet.

**A sub-agent is its parent's chat's, and never has more than its parent.** It runs on its parent's provider, model and effort, in the same folder, with its parent's tools or fewer: a tool the parent doesn't have is refused, and nothing starts. Its calls go through the same door and caller as Rufi AI's (ADR 0016), as the chat's, so its saves and briefs show in the chat with Rufi's receipt or line, which names it by its title ("**Saved** · Checking the thyroid results"); they belong to no reply. Its turns have no message of the user's, so a save names the one it rests on, its position as `read_chat` shows it. Rufi AI's own tools are Rufi's current list, given again at each start; a sub-agent keeps the ones it was given.

**The call that starts it names it.** Its id is the `start_agent` call's Rufi id (`<agent>/<conversation>/<call>`), so the same call again finds it and makes no second one. Each step finds what it did before: the agent is made, its task queued, its owner kept in Rufi's database, then it is admitted. A crash before the owner is kept leaves an agent the next start removes (ADR 0024).

**It sees only its task.** Its first conversation starts with the task alone, never the chat. Its instructions are Rufi AI's, then a part of its own: who started it and for what, that its task isn't the user's words, and that its last answer is its result for the agent that started it. What it does shows in Rufi AI's view of it through `read_agent`, not in the chat.

**30 Oct 2026: sub-agents talk back.** Each turn of a sub-agent ends with a **done message** to its parent, written in the same transaction as the turn's end: a steer from the sub-agent with its title and id and its whole answer, with any questions and their options in plain words, or how it stopped or failed (`about`: done, asked, stopped, failed). It goes into the parent's running turn, or starts one: it is the one way a sub-agent's result reaches its parent. Rufi AI's reply for one keeps its cause (`turns.cause`, migration 0022: the title and why), so its small "why" line stays when the agent database is lost. `send_agent` writes to an agent below or to `"parent"` (`about`: wrote), as a steer or queued, and answers steered, queued or will start the next turn; a parent answers a child this way, as its own answer goes to its parent or the user. `agent_status` only says how they are, and every tool keeps its deadline. Considered: `agent_status` waiting in the caller's turn until one of them ends a turn. It was a second way to the same result: the wait had to keep the done message from going too, and each way missed what the other carried, such as a question, or an agent further below ending its turn.

Considered: a model and effort per sub-agent (they come with the Settings shortlist); a sub-agent's own seed when its conversation is opened afresh (it has its task only; its own history comes with the seed per agent); showing sub-agents' events in the chat (they come with the activity by title). The cost: until done messages and waits come, Rufi AI hears how a sub-agent went only by asking, with `agent_status` or `read_agent`.
