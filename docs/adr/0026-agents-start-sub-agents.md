# 0026 · Agents start sub-agents

**Date:** 30 Oct 2026 · **Status:** accepted

An agent in Rufi's chat starts agents of its own with Rufi's `start_agent` tool, for a task that can run on its own: a **sub-agent**, with a title and its whole task as its first message. It runs at once and goes on after the reply that started it ends. `agent_status` says how the agents below the caller are (no wait yet), and `read_agent` reads the caller's own conversation or one below it, a page of turns at a time. A sub-agent can start its own, to any depth. It is the one kind: Codex's own sub-agents (`spawn_agent` and its tools) are off in the chat's Codex (`[agents] enabled = false`, `multi_agent_v2 = false`), as they are for the reader. These are tools of the agent system (ADR 0024): Rufi's chat does them there (`server/sub-agents.ts`), and screens don't show sub-agents yet.

**A sub-agent is its parent's chat's, and never has more than its parent.** It runs on its parent's provider, model and effort, in the same folder, with its parent's tools or fewer: a tool the parent doesn't have is refused, and nothing starts. Its calls go through the same door and caller as Rufi AI's (ADR 0016), as the chat's, so its saves and briefs show in the chat with Rufi's receipt or line, which names it by its title ("**Saved** · Checking the thyroid results"); they belong to no reply. Its turns have no message of the user's, so a save names the one it rests on, its position as `read_chat` shows it. Rufi AI's own tools are Rufi's current list, given again at each start; a sub-agent keeps the ones it was given.

**The call that starts it names it.** Its id is the `start_agent` call's Rufi id (`<agent>/<conversation>/<call>`), so the same call again finds it and makes no second one. Each step finds what it did before: the agent is made, its task queued, its owner kept in Rufi's database, then it is admitted. A crash before the owner is kept leaves an agent the next start removes (ADR 0024).

**It sees only its task.** Its first conversation starts with the task alone, never the chat. Its instructions are Rufi AI's, then a part of its own: who started it and for what, that its task isn't the user's words, and that its last answer is its result for the agent that started it. What it does shows in Rufi AI's view of it through `read_agent`, not in the chat.

**30 Oct 2026: sub-agents talk back.** Each turn of a sub-agent ends with a **done message** to its parent, written in the same transaction as the turn's end: a steer from the sub-agent with its title and id and its whole answer, with any questions and their options in plain words, or how it stopped or failed (`about`: done, asked, stopped, failed). It goes into the parent's running turn, or starts one: it is the one way a sub-agent's result reaches its parent. Rufi AI's reply for one keeps its cause (`turns.cause`, migration 0022: the title and why), so its small "why" line stays when the agent database is lost. `send_agent` writes to an agent below or to `"parent"` (`about`: wrote), as a steer or queued, and answers steered, queued or will start the next turn; a parent answers a child this way, as its own answer goes to its parent or the user. `agent_status` only says how they are, and every tool keeps its deadline. Considered: `agent_status` waiting in the caller's turn until one of them ends a turn. It was a second way to the same result: the wait had to keep the done message from going too, and each way missed what the other carried, such as a question, or an agent further below ending its turn.

Considered: a model and effort per sub-agent (they come with the Settings shortlist); a sub-agent's own seed when its conversation is opened afresh (it has its task only; its own history comes with the seed per agent); showing sub-agents' events in the chat (they come with the activity by title). The cost: until done messages and waits come, Rufi AI hears how a sub-agent went only by asking, with `agent_status` or `read_agent`.

**31 Oct 2026: an agent reads its whole chat.** `read_agent` reads any agent in the caller's chat, its own too: a sub-agent can build on a sibling's or its parent's work. The chat's agents share its root. `agent_status` still lists only the agents below the caller, and `send_agent` still writes only to one below or to the parent.

**31 Oct 2026: Stop, just this agent or with everything under it.** `stop_agent` stops an agent below the caller, or the caller itself, as one operation of the package (`stop(agent, by, { children })`). Just this agent: its turn ends, what waits for it goes on after, and its children keep working; the person's Stop is this, for Rufi AI. With children: every agent below it stops too, and what waits for any of them is held as stopped (the person's messages show "Not sent · Stopped." with Send). Until they have stopped, what one of them sends another, its done message too, is held the same way, so none wakes another; the stopped agent's own result goes to its parent outside. A steer the provider cancels at the Stop goes back first in line for just this agent, and is held as stopped with children. The tool answers at once with the agents that stop, as an agent can stop itself. Rejected: answering after they have stopped (an agent stopping itself would wait on its own turn).

**31 Oct 2026: agents manage waiting messages.** `waiting_messages` lists what waits for the caller and the agents below it, the person's messages and Rufi's notes too, each with its queue, who sent it and any hold, and edits, removes, reorders or steers one; each action answers the new list. The person's messages and Rufi's notes change in Rufi first, through the chat's own queue actions, as the screens change them, with the refusals the person gets; agents' inputs change in the package. Steering a held message sends it: this is how what a Stop with children held goes again. A reorder moves the given messages among the places they had, and the others keep theirs. Any agent may change any message waiting for it or below it, the person's too; Rufi AI's instructions say to change the person's only when they ask. Considered: an agent changing only the messages it sent; it is a TODO before dogfood.

**31 Oct 2026: models from the shortlist.** The person's shortlist in Settings (the `models` setting, model ids in order) decides which models the chat and its agents run on: the chat's menu lists those offered now, `list_models` gives them to an agent with their provider and efforts, and `start_agent` and `set_agent_model` refuse any other with the chat's own line. A child inherits its parent's model and effort unless the start gives others; either way its model must be on the shortlist, so a chat left on a model the person unticked starts no new agent on it. `set_agent_model` moves an agent below the caller to another model, and its effort, from its next turn, and is refused with one line while that agent's turn starts or runs; Rufi AI's own model is the person's choice in the menu, as it is always in a turn when it calls a tool. Rufi passes the provider only the model and effort. Rufi AI's provider is always its model's, also when the agent was made anew (a restore or a move) on the default one. On another provider's model, the package's `configure` moves the agent there: its next turn opens a new conversation on that provider, never resuming the old one, and a sub-agent's new conversation hears its own last 10 turns (what each was sent, by whom, and its result), never the chat; Rufi AI's hears the chat's last messages, as before. Each turn keeps its model and effort. Considered: refusing a change while an input is with the provider too; a change applies at the next turn's start anyway.

**31 Oct 2026: four models, always there.** The shortlist goes, and so does asking the providers for their models. Rufi has four models, fixed in code and shared by the server and the screens: GPT-6-Astra and GPT-6.1-Sol on Codex, Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 on Claude, each with the same four efforts (low, medium, high, xhigh). The menu always shows the four; a provider that isn't signed in shows its two greyed, with its sign-in. `list_models` goes: `start_agent` and `set_agent_model` name the four models and efforts in their schema, and refuse a model whose provider isn't signed in in one line. A chat stored on another model moves to GPT-6-Astra (migration 0025). Mahesh: "These are the four models only. They should be available. I don't need to select it." Considered: keeping the providers' lists for the menu (the list came slow and changed under the person; the four are what Rufi is tested on).

**31 Oct 2026: sub-agents work beside the chat, and talk to Rufi AI in it.** Rufi AI's sub-agents at work are chips in a row above the message box, and one opens in a pop-up with its own Activity and Stop (by the person, with everything under it, through a route that refuses an agent of another chat). When Rufi AI takes a message from one of its own sub-agents (a turn starts with it, or it is steered into a running one), Rufi writes it into the chat at that point, as an entry of its own (kind `agent`): the agent, its name then, why it came, and its own words. The words are kept apart where they are made (`queue.said` in the agent database), never cut out of Rufi's line, which Rufi AI still gets with the agent's id. The package's event that hands the message over carries the message, so the entry is written from the event alone, in the same transaction as Rufi's cursor, by the input's id: a crash can't lose or double it. Messages between deeper agents stay in their pop-ups. The small "why" line and `turns.cause` go (migration 0024; nobody uses Rufi yet, so older replies lose that line). Considered: the agents' work folded into Rufi AI's Activity, as before, with only a "why" line for their messages (the person never saw what an agent told Rufi AI); a message on the left as a quiet card (Mahesh chose the right, like his own); every working agent in the row (only Rufi AI's own, so a helper's helper stays in its parent's pop-up).
